言行相悖

yán xíng xiāng bèi
Literal: "words, actions, mutually, contradict"
Home › Morality › 言行相悖

📖 Meaning

Speaking and acting in contradictory ways. It describes someone whose words do not match their deeds, revealing a fundamental inconsistency between what they profess and what they actually do.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom does not originate from a single classical text or historical anecdote. It is a compound formed from the common terms 言行 (words and deeds) and 相悖 (mutually contradictory), both deeply rooted in Chinese philosophical tradition. The ideal of unity between words and actions was a recurring theme throughout Chinese intellectual history, from Confucius's emphasis on sincerity in the Analects to later Neo-Confucian moral philosophy, which stressed that a person of integrity must never say one thing and do another.

💡 Example Usage

他整日高谈诚信，自己却言行相悖，实在令人失望。

He talks endlessly about integrity, yet his own words and deeds contradict each other, which is truly disappointing.
