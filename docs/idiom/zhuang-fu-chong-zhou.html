撞府冲州

zhuàng fǔ chōng zhōu
Literal: "ram, prefecture, charge, prefecture"
Home › Lifestyle › 撞府冲州

📖 Meaning

Literally 'charging through prefectures and ramming into cities,' it originally referred to wandering from town to town as a traveling entertainer or merchant running the regional circuits. By extension, it describes someone with rich life experience who has seen much of the world.

📜 Origin Story

The phrase originates from the Yuan dynasty playwright Guan Hanqing's tragedy 'The Injustice to Dou E' (窦娥冤), first act. In the scene, the character Dou E reproaches her mother-in-law, Mistress Cai, for dragging her around through various prefectures and cities, implying a life of aimless wandering and hardship. The expression evokes the image of itinerant performers or peddlers who roamed from one administrative seat to another across the empire.

💡 Example Usage

他年轻时撞府冲州，在外闯荡多年，见过不少世面。

In his youth he traveled through countless towns and cities, wandering far from home for years and gaining a wealth of worldly experience.
