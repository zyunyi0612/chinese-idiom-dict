一路福星

yī lù fú xīng
Literal: "one, road, fortune, star"
Home › Communication › 一路福星

📖 Meaning

Originally referred to a virtuous official who brought blessings to the people under his jurisdiction, likened to an auspicious star that brings good fortune. It later became a polite expression wishing someone a safe and pleasant journey. The idiom conveys the hope that good luck will accompany a traveler along the way.

📜 Origin Story

The idiom originates from the Song Dynasty, where '路' (lù) referred to an administrative district rather than a road, and '福星' (fú xīng) denotes Jupiter (岁星), traditionally regarded as an auspicious star. In his 'Biography of Xianyu Zijun' (鲜于子骏行状), the scholar Qin Guan lamented the loss of such a benevolent official, writing that 'the fortune star has departed, how could we get a hundred Zijun posted across the realm?' Song-era writer Dai Yi similarly praised good governance in his 'Congratulatory Letter to Chen Daizhi,' associating fortune stars with the songs of the people along the road. Over time, the phrase shifted from praising virtuous officials to a courteous blessing for travelers.

💡 Example Usage

老朋友要远行，我祝他一路福星，平安到达目的地。

As my old friend was about to depart on a long journey, I wished him safe travels under the protection of an auspicious star, arriving peacefully at his destination.
