风花雪夜

fēng huā xuě yè
Literal: "wind, flower, snow, night"
Home › Quality › 风花雪夜

📖 Meaning

Originally referred to the natural scenery — spring wind, flowers, snow, and moonlit nights — most commonly depicted in classical poetry and prose. By extension, it is used to criticize writing that piles up ornate, flowery language while being poor and hollow in actual content. It describes artful words that lack real substance.

📜 Origin Story

No recorded origin story exists for this idiom, and dictionaries generally note its provenance as unknown. It is widely regarded as a variant of the more common idiom 风花雪月 (wind, flower, snow, moon), which originally denoted the beautiful seasonal scenes that classical poets most loved to describe. As later writers overused such imagery merely for ornament, the phrase gradually came to mean ornate, flowery writing that was rich in words but poor in substance. Because no specific dynasty, text, or author can be credited with coining it, its origin is listed simply as none.

💡 Example Usage

我们对过去那些描写风花雪夜的作品，要用历史唯物主义的观点加以分析。

We must analyze past works that describe such romanticized scenery from a historical materialist perspective.
