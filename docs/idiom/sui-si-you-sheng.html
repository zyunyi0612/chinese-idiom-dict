虽死犹生

suī sǐ yóu shēng
Literal: "though, dead, as if, alive"
Home › Philosophy › 虽死犹生

📖 Meaning

Though physically dead, one's spirit lives on and their legacy endures as a model for others. It can also describe someone who dies without regret or unfulfilled wishes, making their death feel no different from being alive.

📜 Origin Story

The idiom derives from a statement by a woman named Wenji (文姬) recorded in Chang Qu's (常璩) 'Records of Heroines of Hanzhong' (《汉中士女志》) during the Jin dynasty. She declared that her late father had been a loyal official of the Han dynasty, so that 'the day of his death was like a year of life' (虽死之日，犹生之年) — meaning he died with honor and without regret. Over time the phrase was condensed into the four-character idiom 虽死犹生. It came to signify that a noble person's influence and spirit persist long after they are gone.

💡 Example Usage

他为国捐躯，虽死犹生，永远活在人民心中。

He sacrificed his life for the country; though dead, he lives on forever in the hearts of the people.
