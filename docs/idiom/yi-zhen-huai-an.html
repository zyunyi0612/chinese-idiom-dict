一枕槐安

yī zhěn huái ān
Literal: "one, pillow, pagoda tree, peace"
Home › Philosophy › 一枕槐安

📖 Meaning

Refers to a dream or illusory experience. It metaphorically describes a fleeting happiness that turns out to be empty upon waking. The idiom evokes the idea that worldly success and pleasure may be nothing more than a passing dream.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom derives from Tang dynasty dream-literature. The term '槐安' (Huai'an) refers to the Kingdom of Huai'an, a dream realm beneath a pagoda tree, as recounted in Li Gongzuo's tale '南柯太守传' (The Governor of Nanke). In the story, a disillusioned scholar named Chunyu Fen fell asleep beneath an old pagoda tree and dreamed he was invited to the Kingdom of Huai'an, where he served as governor for decades, enjoying wealth, rank, and a loving family. Upon waking, he discovered the entire kingdom was merely an ant colony nesting in the tree's roots. The phrase '一枕槐安' thus came to symbolize the vanity and emptiness of illusory worldly achievements.

💡 Example Usage

他苦心经营的商业帝国最终不过是一枕槐安，转眼便化为乌有。

The business empire he painstakingly built turned out to be nothing but an empty dream, vanishing into thin air in the blink of an eye.
