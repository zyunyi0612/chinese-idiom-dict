揎拳掳袖

xuān quán lǔ xiù
Literal: "roll up fists, bare sleeves"
Home › Action › 揎拳掳袖

📖 Meaning

Rolling up one's sleeves and clenching one's fists to expose the arms. It describes a person preparing to start a fight or take physical action. The image conveys aggression and readiness for violence.

📜 Origin Story

The idiom originates from the Yuan dynasty zaju play 'Ma Danyang' by playwright Yang Jingxian. In one passage, the character of a music official is criticized for bullying the good and acting lawlessly, described as 'rolling up sleeves and clenching fists to commit violence' (揎拳捋袖行凶暴). The phrase vividly depicts a belligerent posture. Over time, '捋' was replaced or paired with '掳' in common usage, and the expression became a standard idiom for someone on the verge of a physical altercation.

💡 Example Usage

他一听这话，立刻揎拳掳袖，准备大打出手。

Hearing this, he immediately rolled up his sleeves and clenched his fists, ready to start a brawl.
