有来有往

yǒu lái yǒu wǎng
Literal: "have, come, have, go"
Home › Communication › 有来有往

📖 Meaning

Refers to reciprocal and equal action between two parties. The specific meaning varies by context—it can describe mutual exchange in communication, trade, favors, or learning. The idiom emphasizes that relationships work best when both sides participate equally.

📜 Origin Story

The phrase appears in Chapter 11 of Tao Xingzhi's 1934 work 'How to Be a Little Teacher' (怎样做小先生). Tao Xingzhi was a prominent modern Chinese educator who championed popular and democratic education. He wrote that when one humbly seeks advice from another, the other will also humbly seek advice in return, concluding that 'the best education is reciprocal exchange.' The idiom encapsulates his philosophy of mutual, two-way learning.

💡 Example Usage

朋友之间应该有来有往，不能总是一方在付出。

Between friends there should be a mutual give-and-take; one side cannot always be the one giving.
