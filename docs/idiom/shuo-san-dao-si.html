说三道四

shuō sān dào sì
Literal: "say three, speak four"
Home › Communication › 说三道四

📖 Meaning

To make irresponsible and reckless remarks about others, often behind their backs. It refers to gossiping, nitpicking, or casually criticizing people and situations without any real basis or authority.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom first appeared in 'Tongsu Bian' (A Compilation of Common Sayings), a Qing Dynasty work by scholar Cui Hao. The original passage admonishes against imitating those who 'do not know dawn from dusk, wander through villages, and speak carelessly of this and that.' The phrase uses the numbers three and four metaphorically to suggest idle chatter about anything and everything, implying the speaker has no real substance or right to comment.

💡 Example Usage

他什么都不懂，却总爱对别人的工作说三道四。

He knows nothing about it, yet he's always making irresponsible remarks about other people's work.
