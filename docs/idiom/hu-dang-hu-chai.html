虎党狐侪

hǔ dǎng hú chái
Literal: "tiger, party, fox, companions"
Home › Morality › 虎党狐侪

📖 Meaning

Literally 'tiger parties and fox companions.' It refers to associates who are both fierce and cunning, like tigers and foxes. The idiom describes a group of vicious and deceitful people who band together.

📜 Origin Story

The idiom originates from Ming Dynasty playwright Kang Hai's drama 'The Wolf of Zhongshan' (中山狼), specifically in the second act. In the passage, a character remarks that behind a refined brocade bag of poems, there may hide vicious and cunning companions like tigers and foxes. The metaphor uses the tiger for ferocity and the fox for cunning, together characterizing a group of wicked, deceitful associates. The work itself is a famous allegorical tale about the treachery of the Zhongshan wolf and the folly of misplaced compassion.

💡 Example Usage

他整日与那些虎党狐侪混在一起，最终也走上了犯罪的道路。

He spent his days mingling with those vicious and cunning associates, and eventually went down the path of crime himself.
