怪声怪气

guài shēng guài qì
Literal: "strange, voice, strange, tone"
Home › Communication › 怪声怪气

📖 Meaning

Describes a voice, tone, or singing style that sounds comical, bizarre, or unpleasantly odd. It often criticizes an affected, unnatural, or theatrical manner of speaking. The phrase conveys disapproval of someone deliberately distorting their voice for effect.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom originates from Lu Xun's essay collection Er Xin Ji (二心集, 1930), specifically the essay 'Propaganda and Performance' (宣传与做戏), written during the Republic of China era. Lu Xun used it to satirize pretentious individuals who styled themselves after figures like Lord Guan or Lin Daiyu, performing in exaggerated, unnatural tones. The phrase vividly captures affected, distorted vocal delivery meant to impress but sounding merely ridiculous.

💡 Example Usage

他怪声怪气地学着老师说话，逗得全班同学大笑。

He mimicked the teacher in a weird, affected tone, making the whole class laugh.
