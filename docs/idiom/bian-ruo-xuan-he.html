辨若悬河

biàn ruò xuán hé
Literal: "debate, like, suspended river"
Home › Communication › 辨若悬河

📖 Meaning

Describes someone who speaks with extraordinary eloquence and fluency, as if words pour from their mouth like a rushing river. The character 辨 is interchangeable with 辩, meaning to argue or debate. It is essentially equivalent to the more common idiom 口若悬河.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom originates from the Ming Dynasty scholar and statesman Zhang Juzheng's essay 'On Reading in the Hanlin Academy' (翰林院读书说). In the essay, Zhang describes an ideal scholar whose eloquence flows like a suspended river and whose literary embellishment rivals the craftsmanship of spring. He further compares this person's rhetorical talent to historical literary masters Zou Yang and Mei Sheng, and places him on equal footing with Lu Ji and Xie Lingyun. The passage served as a model of scholarly excellence for Hanlin academicians.

💡 Example Usage

他在辩论赛上辨若悬河，令对手毫无招架之力。

During the debate competition, he argued with such eloquence that his opponents were left with no way to fight back.
