寄兴寓情

jì xìng yù qíng
Literal: "entrust, interest, contain, feeling"
Home › Emotion › 寄兴寓情

📖 Meaning

To entrust and embody one's interests, moods, and feelings within a work of art or writing. It describes creative pieces in which the author's private emotions and aesthetic inclinations are subtly woven into the subject matter rather than stated outright.

📜 Origin Story

The idiom traces back to the Eastern Jin dynasty calligrapher Wang Xizhi's 'Preface to the Orchid Pavilion Collection' (兰亭集序), which describes sages 'entrusting their feelings to what they take pleasure in, letting their spirits roam beyond the bounds of physical form' (或因寄所托，放浪形骸之外). From this reflection on how people lodge their hearts in the things they love, later literary criticism distilled the phrase 寄兴寓情 to describe the practice of infusing creative works with the author's personal interest and sentiment. It has since become a standard term for the expressive, emotive quality prized in classical Chinese poetry and painting.

💡 Example Usage

作者寄兴寓情于山水，诗作自然真挚动人。

The author lodges his interests and feelings in the mountains and rivers, making his poems natural, sincere, and deeply moving.
