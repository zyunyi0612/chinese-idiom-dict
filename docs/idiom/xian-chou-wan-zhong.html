闲愁万种

xián chóu wàn zhǒng
Literal: "idle melancholy, ten thousand kinds"
Home › Emotion › 闲愁万种

📖 Meaning

Refers to countless worries that cannot be named or expressed. Describes someone with an empty mind who is overly sentimental, or whose heart is filled with vague, overwhelming sorrow.

📜 Origin Story

The phrase originates from 'Zhang Sheng Boils the Sea' (《张生煮海》), a zaju play written by Li Haogu during the Yuan dynasty (1271–1368). It appears in the first act in the line '真乃是消磨了闲愁万种', meaning 'truly, this dispels ten thousand kinds of idle melancholy.' The idiom captures the sentiment of vague, unnameable sorrow that overwhelms an empty-hearted, over-sensitive person. It reflects the expressive, lyrical style characteristic of Yuan dynasty drama.

💡 Example Usage

秋雨绵绵的夜晚，闲愁万种，让人辗转难眠。

On a drizzly autumn night, with ten thousand kinds of idle sorrows in my heart, I toss and turn, unable to fall asleep.
