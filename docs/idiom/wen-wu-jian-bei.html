文武兼备

wén wǔ jiān bèi
Literal: "civil, martial, both, possess"
Home › Quality › 文武兼备

📖 Meaning

To be proficient in both literary and military arts; a person who excels in scholarly pursuits as well as martial skills. This idiom describes a well-rounded individual who is equally capable with pen and sword, embodying the ideal of the complete talent in traditional Chinese culture.

📜 Origin Story

The concept of balancing civil and martial prowess dates back to the Zhou Dynasty, where the ideal gentleman was expected to master the Six Arts, including archery and charioteering alongside rites and music. While no single text is attributed as the definitive source, the idiom crystallized in later dynastic records and historical writings to praise officials and generals who demonstrated excellence in both governance and warfare. Figures such as the Three Kingdoms strategist Zhuge Liang and the Tang Dynasty general Li Jing are often cited as exemplars of this well-rounded ideal.

💡 Example Usage

这位将军文武兼备，既能运筹帷幄，又能冲锋陷阵。

This general is accomplished in both civil and martial arts, capable of devising strategies in the command tent as well as charging into battle.
