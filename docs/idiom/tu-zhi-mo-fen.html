涂脂抹粉

tú zhī mǒ fěn
Literal: "apply rouge, wipe powder"
Home › Method › 涂脂抹粉

📖 Meaning

To apply rouge and powder to one's face. It literally refers to a woman putting on makeup, but is commonly used metaphorically to mean whitewashing or glossing over something ugly or shameful to make it appear attractive.

📜 Origin Story

The phrase comes from Ling Mengchu's "Slapping the Table in Amazement" (二刻拍案惊奇), a collection of vernacular short stories from the Ming Dynasty. In the story 'Magistrate's Lady Zhao Cleverly Sends Huang Citrus,' the expression describes a woman who applies makeup and deliberately flaunts her charms to seduce wealthy young men. Over time, the phrase evolved from its literal sense of cosmetic beautification into a common metaphor for covering up the true, often unsavory nature of something through superficial embellishment.

💡 Example Usage

这篇报道对事故原因涂脂抹粉，回避了真正的问题。

The report whitewashed the cause of the accident and sidestepped the real issue.
