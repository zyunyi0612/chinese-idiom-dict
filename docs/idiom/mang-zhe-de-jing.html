盲者得镜

máng zhě dé jìng
Literal: "blind, person, gets, mirror"
Home › Philosophy › 盲者得镜

📖 Meaning

Describes something that cannot fulfill its proper function because it is in the wrong hands or context. It also serves as a metaphor for talents being wasted or buried when not given the right opportunity.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom originates from the Huainanzi (淮南子·人间训), a philosophical compendium compiled under Liu An of the Western Han dynasty. The original passage reads, 'A blind person obtains a mirror and uses it to cover a cup.' Since a mirror's purpose is to reflect one's appearance, a blind person—who cannot see—cannot use it as intended and instead repurposes it as a lid. The text uses this image to illustrate how objects lose their value when placed in unsuitable circumstances, and by extension how talent goes to waste when not matched with the right opportunity.

💡 Example Usage

让不识字的农夫管理图书馆，无异于盲者得镜。

Assigning an illiterate farmer to manage a library is like giving a mirror to a blind man — a complete waste.
