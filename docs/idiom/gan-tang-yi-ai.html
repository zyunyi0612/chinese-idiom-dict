甘棠遗爱

gān táng yí ài
Literal: "sweet pear tree, leave behind, love"
Home › Leadership › 甘棠遗爱

📖 Meaning

A metaphor for the lasting gratitude and affection the people feel toward a departed local official who governed with benevolence. It originally referred to the sweet pear tree the people would not cut down because a beloved official had once rested beneath it. The phrase is used to praise officials whose virtuous rule is remembered after they leave office.

📜 Origin Story

The idiom comes from the poem 'Gan Tang' (甘棠) in the 'Odes of Zhou and the South' (周南) section of the Classic of Poetry (诗经), dating to the Western Zhou Dynasty. According to tradition, the Duke of Shao (召伯, Shaobo) was a virtuous minister who, while inspecting the realm, once rested and heard cases of law under a sweet pear tree. After he departed, the people cherished the tree so deeply that they forbade anyone from cutting or trimming it, as a token of their love and respect for his just governance. Over time the sheltering tree became a symbol of the enduring affection left behind by a good official.

💡 Example Usage

这位老县长离任多年，百姓仍感念其恩德，可谓甘棠遗爱。

Years after the old county magistrate left office, the people still cherish his kindness—a true case of benevolence remembered.
