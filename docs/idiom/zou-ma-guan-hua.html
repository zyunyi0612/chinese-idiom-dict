走马观花

zǒu mǎ guān huā
Literal: "ride, horse, look, flower"
Home › Method › 走马观花

📖 Meaning

To look at flowers while riding a horse — meaning to give something a quick, superficial glance without taking in the details. It describes a hasty, cursory observation that misses the finer points.

📜 Origin Story

The idiom comes from a poem by the Tang dynasty poet Meng Jiao (孟郊) titled '登科后' (After Passing the Imperial Examination), written in 796 AD. Having finally passed the civil service exam at age forty-six, Meng Jiao rode his horse through the streets of Chang'an in high spirits, writing: '春风得意马蹄疾，一日看尽长安花' — 'Riding fast on spring breeze, I viewed all the flowers of Chang'an in a single day.' Later generations adapted this into the idiom '走马观花' to describe a rushed, superficial experience of things.

💡 Example Usage

这次欧洲之行时间太短，不过是走马观花而已，很多景点都没来得及仔细游览。

The European trip was so short that it was just a quick glance — many attractions didn't get the thorough visit they deserved.
