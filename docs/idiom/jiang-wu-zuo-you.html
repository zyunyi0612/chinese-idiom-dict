将无作有

jiāng wú zuò yǒu
Literal: "take, nothing, as, having"
Home › Communication › 将无作有

📖 Meaning

To treat something that does not exist as if it were real; to fabricate a falsehood or make up a story out of thin air. It describes the act of deliberately inventing facts or events where none exist, often with deceptive intent.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom originates from the Yuan dynasty zaju play 'Li Kui Bears the Thorn' (李逵负荆) by the playwright Kang Jinzhi. In Act One, a character warns another not to fabricate falsehoods, saying: 'Do not take what is nothing and make it something; just follow what came before and after.' The line cautions against inventing nonexistent offenses. Over time, the phrase was condensed into the four-character idiom '将无作有,' used to describe the act of conjuring false claims where no truth exists.

💡 Example Usage

他总爱将无作有，编些没影的事来挑拨离间。

He always likes to fabricate things out of thin air, making up baseless stories to sow discord among people.
