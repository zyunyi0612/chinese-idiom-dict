凤泊鸾飘

fèng bó luán piāo
Literal: "phoenix adrift, luan floating"
Home › Status › 凤泊鸾飘

📖 Meaning

A metaphor for talented individuals whose abilities go unrecognized, leaving them to wander restlessly without a stable position or purpose. The idiom evokes the image of noble mythical birds—the phoenix and the luan—drifting aimlessly, symbolizing the plight of the gifted but unappreciated.

📜 Origin Story

The idiom first appears in a preface written by Qing dynasty scholar Quan Zuwang (全祖望, 1705–1755), a noted historian and poet. In his preface, he laments how even the finest talents, like phoenixes and luan birds, end up drifting and scattered, their potential wasted. The phrase was later popularized by poet Liu Yazi (柳亚子, 1887–1958), who used it in his poem 'Chong Ti' (重题) to reflect on the hardships faced by scholars and revolutionaries of his era.

💡 Example Usage

凤泊鸾飘半生，他始终不改初心。

Adrift like a phoenix for half his life, he never wavered from his original aspirations.
