执鞭坠镫

zhí biān zhuì dèng
Literal: "hold, whip, let fall, stirrup"
Home › Attitude › 执鞭坠镫

📖 Meaning

This idiom literally refers to holding someone's whip and adjusting their stirrups when they mount a horse, an act of attentive service. It expresses a willingness to serve and follow someone with complete devotion and loyalty. It is commonly used to convey eagerness to assist a leader or repay a benefactor's kindness.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom originates from 'Water Margin' (水浒传) by Shi Nai'an of the Ming Dynasty, Chapter 67. In the story, a character who had been saved expresses deep gratitude and declares that if he could serve his savior by holding his whip and adjusting his stirrups, even as a lowly foot soldier, it would be a great fortune to repay the life-saving kindness. The phrase has since become a classical expression of wholehearted devotion and willingness to follow someone faithfully.

💡 Example Usage

蒙将军厚恩，在下愿执鞭坠镫，誓死追随左右。

Having received the general's great kindness, I am willing to serve him faithfully and follow him to the death.
