者也之乎

zhě yě zhī hū
Literal: "that which, indeed, of, how"
Home › Communication › 者也之乎

📖 Meaning

The four characters 者、也、之、乎 are all common grammatical particles in classical Chinese. The idiom describes someone who speaks or writes in a pedantic, overly literary manner, chewing on words to show off learning. It is used sarcastically to mock pretentious scholarly affectation.

📜 Origin Story

The idiom originates from the Yuan dynasty play Recommending the Fu Stele (荐福碑) by the playwright Ma Zhiyuan, in its first act. The line reads: 'Who buys the Changmen Fu with gold — it's hardly worth a thing with all this zhe-ye-zhi-hu.' The Changmen Fu was a famous rhapsody composed by the Han dynasty poet Sima Xiangru. By stringing together four common classical Chinese grammatical particles, the phrase satirizes pedantic scholars who cling to archaic literary forms and empty rhetoric.

💡 Example Usage

他满口者也之乎，实际却什么真学问也没有。

He's full of classical rhetoric, but actually has no real learning at all.
