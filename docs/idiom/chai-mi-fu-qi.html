柴米夫妻

chái mǐ fū qī
Literal: "firewood, rice, husband, wife"
Home › Lifestyle › 柴米夫妻

📖 Meaning

A couple bound together by the basic necessities of life such as firewood and rice. It refers to a husband and wife united by practical material needs rather than romantic love, typically living in humble or impoverished circumstances. The phrase highlights the pragmatic foundation of many traditional marriages.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom originates from a Chinese folk proverb: '香烟兄弟，柴米夫妻' (Incense brothers, firewood-and-rice husband and wife). The proverb contrasts two kinds of relationships: brotherhood sealed by burning incense together, and marriage sustained by sharing the daily necessities of firewood and rice. It reflects the traditional Chinese view that marriage was often a practical partnership built on meeting basic survival needs rather than on romantic ideals. Over time, the phrase '柴米夫妻' alone came to denote a poor couple whose union is rooted in shared material struggle.

💡 Example Usage

他们本是柴米夫妻，日子虽清贫却也安稳。

They were a couple bound by daily necessities; though life was poor, it was peaceful and stable.
