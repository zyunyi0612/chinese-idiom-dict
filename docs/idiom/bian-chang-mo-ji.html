鞭长莫及

biān cháng mò jí
Literal: "whip, long, cannot, reach"
Home › Distance › 鞭长莫及

📖 Meaning

Originally meaning that even a long whip cannot reach the horse's belly. It is now used as a metaphor for being too far away for one's power or influence to reach, despite the desire to help or intervene.

📜 Origin Story

The idiom comes from the Zuo Zhuan (左传·宣公十五年), dating to the Spring and Autumn period. In 594 BC, the state of Chu attacked the state of Song, which sent envoys to the state of Jin pleading for military aid. Jin's minister Bo Zong (伯宗) advised against intervention, arguing that Jin was too distant to effectively aid Song. He used the analogy that even with a long whip, one cannot strike the horse's belly (虽鞭之长，不及马腹), meaning strength has its limits when distance is too great. Jin ultimately did not send troops, and Song was forced to make terms with Chu.

💡 Example Usage

孩子在外求学，父母再担心也鞭长莫及。

With their child studying far from home, the parents worried but were powerless to help from afar.
