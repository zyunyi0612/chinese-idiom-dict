黄童皓首

huáng tóng hào shǒu
Literal: "yellow child, white head"
Home › Variety › 黄童皓首

📖 Meaning

This idiom refers to people of all ages, from young children to the elderly. The yellow-haired child represents the young, while the white head represents the old. It describes a gathering or situation involving people across all generations.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom is a variant of the more common expression 黄童白叟. The term combines 黄童, referring to young children whose hair has a yellowish tint, and 皓首, meaning white-haired elders. Such expressions appear in classical Chinese literature to describe the full spectrum of ages in a population, often used when depicting scenes where entire communities come together regardless of age.

💡 Example Usage

每逢庙会，黄童皓首皆倾城而出，热闹非凡。

During every temple fair, people of all ages, from young children to white-haired elders, would pour out of the city in a bustling crowd.
