驾鹤西游

jià hè xī yóu
Literal: "ride, crane, west, travel"
Home › Condition › 驾鹤西游

📖 Meaning

A euphemism for dying or passing away. The imagery evokes a person riding a crane to journey to the Western Paradise, implying a peaceful and graceful death. It is used as a polite and literary way to refer to someone's passing.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom blends Daoist and Buddhist imagery. In Daoist mythology, immortals are often depicted riding cranes to ascend to the heavens. The 'west' refers to the Western Pure Land (西方极乐世界), the Buddhist paradise of Amitabha. Combining these two traditions, the phrase paints death as a serene journey westward aboard a crane. Over time it became a refined literary euphemism for death in classical Chinese writing.

💡 Example Usage

老先生去年驾鹤西游了，享年九旬。

The elderly gentleman passed away last year at the age of ninety.
