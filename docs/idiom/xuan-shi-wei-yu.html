炫石为玉

xuàn shí wéi yù
Literal: "flaunt, stone, as, jade"
Home › Morality › 炫石为玉

📖 Meaning

To pass off stone as jade, flaunting something worthless as if it were precious. It is a metaphor for words not matching actions — claiming one thing while doing another, or advertising quality while delivering inferior goods.

📜 Origin Story

The phrase traces to the Han dynasty scholar Yang Xiong, who wrote in his work 'Fa Yan' (Model Sayings), chapter 'Wen Dao' (Asking about the Way): 'Those who flaunt jade but sell stone — are they not deceitful?' Later, the Song dynasty Neo-Confucian Cheng Hao echoed the idea in his 'Discussion of the Distinction between the Kingly Way and the Hegemonic Way,' writing: 'If one seeks to achieve the kingly way with the heart of a hegemon, that is flaunting stone as jade.' Over time the idiom came to describe hypocrisy — claiming virtue or quality while practicing the opposite.

💡 Example Usage

这家店炫石为玉，把劣质品当珍品出售。

This shop flaunts stone as jade, selling shoddy goods as if they were treasures.
