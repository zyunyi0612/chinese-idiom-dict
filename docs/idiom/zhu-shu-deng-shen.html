著述等身

zhù shù děng shēn
Literal: "writings, equal, body height"
Home › Learning › 著述等身

📖 Meaning

One's written works pile up to equal one's own height. It describes a person who has authored an enormous volume of books and writings. The idiom is also written as 著作等身.

📜 Origin Story

The phrase originates from the Qing dynasty scholar Ji Yun's (纪昀) work 'Notes from the Thatched Abode of Close Observations' (阅微草堂笔记), specifically the 'Luan yang Summer Records' section. In the passage, Ji Yun discusses how scholars, even those whose prolific writings equal their own height and whose fame surpasses their era, are still not automatically admitted into the highest ranks of Confucian sanctity. The idiom thus originally carried a tone of humility, cautioning that sheer quantity of scholarship alone does not guarantee ultimate recognition. Over time it came to be used more straightforwardly to praise someone's extraordinary literary productivity.

💡 Example Usage

这位学者一生著述等身，为后人留下了宝贵的学术财富。

This scholar wrote prolifically throughout his life, producing a body of work as tall as himself, and left behind a precious academic legacy for future generations.
