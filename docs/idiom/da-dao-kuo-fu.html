大刀阔斧

dà dāo kuò fǔ
Literal: "big, knife, broad, axe"
Home › Determination › 大刀阔斧

📖 Meaning

Originally described wielding a large broad-axe to hack at enemies in battle. It is now used metaphorically to describe acting with boldness, decisiveness, and vigor. It often characterizes a person or organization that handles matters resolutely and without hesitation.

📜 Origin Story

The phrase originates from the Ming dynasty novel 'Water Margin' (水浒全传) by Shi Nai'an. In Chapter 118, it describes a military scene where troops are urged to raid an enemy camp, charging in with large swords and broad-axes. The vivid battlefield imagery of hacking with heavy weapons gave rise to the figurative meaning of handling affairs with boldness and resolve.

💡 Example Usage

新厂长上任后大刀阔斧地改革了生产线。

After taking office, the new factory director carried out sweeping reforms to the production line.
