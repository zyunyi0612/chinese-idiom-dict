弄瓦之喜

nòng wǎ zhī xǐ
Literal: "play with, clay spindle whorl, of, joy"
Home › Status › 弄瓦之喜

📖 Meaning

This idiom is a conventional phrase of congratulations used when a family has a newborn daughter. It draws on the ancient custom of giving a baby girl a clay spindle whorl (瓦) to play with, symbolizing the hope that she will one day excel at weaving and other household crafts. Today it appears chiefly in formal or traditional congratulations.

📜 Origin Story

弄瓦之喜 comes from the poem 'Sigan' (斯干) in the Lesser Court Hymns (小雅) section of the Classic of Poetry (《诗经》), the earliest anthology of Chinese verse, compiled during the Zhou dynasty. The relevant line reads: 'When a daughter is born, she is laid to sleep on the ground, wrapped in swaddling clothes, and given a clay whorl to play with' (乃生女子，载寝之地，载衣之裼，载弄之瓦). In ancient times, 瓦 referred to a clay spindle whorl used in spinning thread, and giving it to a baby girl expressed the wish that she would grow up to be diligent in weaving and other household crafts. Later writers turned this image into a set phrase for congratulating a family on the birth of a daughter, and it remains the female counterpart of 弄璋之喜, which celebrates the birth of a son.

💡 Example Usage

李府喜得千金，全家上下喜气洋洋，我在此恭贺弄瓦之喜。

The Li family has welcomed a daughter, and everyone in the household is overjoyed — I extend my congratulations on this happy occasion.
