士死知己

shì sǐ zhī jǐ
Literal: "scholar, die, know, self"
Home › Loyalty › 士死知己

📖 Meaning

A person of talent and virtue is willing to sacrifice their life to repay someone who truly understands and appreciates them. It expresses the deep loyalty and gratitude felt toward a kindred spirit who recognizes one's worth. An equivalent saying is 'a gentleman dies for the one who knows him.'

📜 Origin Story

The phrase derives from the classical Chinese concept of 知己 (zhī jǐ), a person who truly understands and appreciates one's character and abilities. This idea is famously articulated in the saying 士为知己者死, attributed to the Warring States period and associated with figures such as Yu Rang (豫让), an assassin of the Jin state who sought to avenge his patron Zhi Bo. The sentiment reflects the deep bonds of loyalty cultivated between a worthy leader and a capable retainer in ancient China. Over time, 士死知己 became a condensed variant expressing the same devotion.

💡 Example Usage

古语云士死知己，他既蒙将军知遇之恩，便愿以性命相报。

As the old saying goes, a scholar dies for the one who knows him; having received the general's recognition, he is willing to repay it with his life.
