魂颠梦倒

hún diān mèng dǎo
Literal: "soul, overturn, dream, invert"
Home › Emotion › 魂颠梦倒

📖 Meaning

An expression meaning to be utterly disoriented and lost in a daze, akin to '神魂颠倒'. It describes a person whose spirit is in turmoil and who has lost normal composure, behaving in a confused and disorderly manner.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom is a variant expression of the more common '神魂颠倒', constructed by pairing 魂 (soul) with 颠 (overturn) and 梦 (dream) with 倒 (invert) in a parallel structure that emphasizes disorientation. It appears in vernacular Chinese fiction and drama of the Ming and Qing dynasties, where it was used to depict characters so bewitched or infatuated—often by love or beauty—that they could not think or act straight. No single canonical source is recorded; it circulated as a literary phrase drawing on the long-standing Chinese trope that intense emotion unsettles the 魂 (soul) and disrupts dreams.

💡 Example Usage

自那夜听她弹了一曲，他便魂颠梦倒，整日心神不宁。

After hearing her play that tune one night, he was utterly bewitched and disoriented, restless all day long.
