士饱马腾

shì bǎo mǎ téng
Literal: "soldiers full, horses prancing"
Home › Condition › 士饱马腾

📖 Meaning

The idiom describes an army that is well-provisioned and brimming with high morale. Soldiers are well-fed and spirited, and even the horses are vigorous and energetic, reflecting a state of peak military readiness.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom originates from Han Yu's 'Pacifying Huaixi Stele' (平淮西碑) in the Tang Dynasty. Han Yu, a renowned Tang scholar and official, praised the imperial army's successful campaign to quell a rebellion in the Huaixi region. The original line reads, 'Soldiers, well-fed, burst into song; horses prance in their stables,' capturing the vibrant scene of a well-supplied military camp. Over time, the phrase was distilled into the four-character idiom to symbolize abundant provisions and soaring morale.

💡 Example Usage

粮草运到前线后，全军士饱马腾，士气大振。

After the supplies reached the front line, the entire army was well-fed and in high spirits, with morale greatly boosted.
