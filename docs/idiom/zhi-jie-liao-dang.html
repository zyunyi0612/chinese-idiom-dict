直截了当

zhí jié liǎo dàng
Literal: "straight, cut, clear, proper"
Home › Communication › 直截了当

📖 Meaning

Straightforward and direct, without beating around the bush. Describes speech or action that gets right to the point without evasion or ambiguity. It conveys clarity, frankness, and decisiveness in handling matters.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom evolved from earlier colloquial usage in vernacular literature during the Ming and Qing dynasties. It appears in works such as the vernacular fiction of the period, where 直截 (straight cut) was combined with 了当 (clear and settled) to describe acting without hesitation or roundaboutness. The phrase reflects a folk linguistic tradition valuing candor and efficiency, contrasting with the elaborate indirectness often found in formal classical prose. It gained wide currency in late imperial vernacular writing and became a standard entry in modern idiom dictionaries.

💡 Example Usage

有话直截了当地说，别拐弯抹角了。

If you have something to say, say it straightforwardly — stop beating around the bush.
