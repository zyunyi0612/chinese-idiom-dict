抉瑕摘衅

jué xiá zhāi xìn
Literal: "pick out flaws, pluck out faults"
Home › Attitude › 抉瑕摘衅

📖 Meaning

To deliberately and meticulously search for faults, blemishes, or weaknesses in someone or something. It describes a hypercritical, nitpicking attitude where one goes out of their way to expose even the smallest imperfections, often with hostile or malicious intent.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom is a compound of two verb-object phrases drawn from classical Chinese vocabulary. '抉瑕' (jué xiá) means to dig out or expose flaws, and '摘衅' (zhāi xìn) means to pluck out or seize upon faults or quarrels. The idiom has no single traceable origin in a specific historical text, figure, or dynasty. It likely emerged as a literary combination during the Ming or Qing dynasties, when parallel-construction four-character idioms were commonly coined by scholars and officials to describe petty fault-finding behavior in bureaucratic or literary circles. It belongs to the same family of critical idioms as '吹毛求疵'.

💡 Example Usage

他整天抉瑕摘衅，同事们都躲着他。

He spends all day nitpicking and finding fault, so his colleagues all avoid him.
