鸾跂鸿惊

luán qí hóng jīng
Literal: "phoenix bird lifts up, wild swan startles"
Home › Skill › 鸾跂鸿惊

📖 Meaning

This idiom describes the soaring and dynamic quality of calligraphy brushwork. It compares the lifted, flying strokes to a mythical phoenix rising on its toes and a wild swan startled into sudden flight. It praises calligraphy that possesses an energetic, elevated, and vividly alive spirit.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom originates from classical Chinese literary criticism of calligraphy, where vivid animal imagery was conventionally used to describe the qualities of masterful brushwork. The mythical luán bird, a phoenix-like creature, standing on tiptoe as if about to soar into the sky evokes a sense of upward grace, while a startled wild swan taking flight suggests sudden, explosive movement. Together these images capture the impression of calligraphy that seems alive with motion and vitality. No single definitive textual source is documented for this phrase.

💡 Example Usage

他的草书鸾跂鸿惊，笔势飞动，令人叹为观止。

His cursive calligraphy soars like a startled phoenix and swan, its brushwork so dynamic it takes one's breath away.
