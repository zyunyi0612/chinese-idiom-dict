飘蓬断梗

piāo péng duàn gěng
Literal: "drifting tumbleweed, broken twig"
Home › Lifestyle › 飘蓬断梗

📖 Meaning

Like tumbleweed blown about by the wind and broken branches carried along by the current, it describes a person who wanders from place to place without a fixed home or any sense of where they will end up. It conveys a life of rootless drifting and unsettled existence.

📜 Origin Story

The idiom originates from a letter by Zong Ze (宗泽), a renowned general of the Song Dynasty, titled 'Letter to Zheng Longtu Requesting a Boat' (《上郑龙图求船书》). In it, Zong Ze wrote that his entire family of about a hundred members were scattered like drifting tumbleweed and broken twigs — one person at the far edge of the sky, another at the distant corner of the earth. The vivid imagery captures the helplessness of families torn apart and displaced by the chaos of war, drifting without roots or destination.

💡 Example Usage

半生飘蓬断梗，他终于在这座小城安定了下来。

After half a lifetime of drifting like tumbleweed and broken twigs, he finally settled down in this small town.
