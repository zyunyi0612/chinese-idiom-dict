柳陌花丛

liǔ mò huā cóng
Literal: "willow, path, flower, thicket"
Home › Lifestyle › 柳陌花丛

📖 Meaning

A classical Chinese euphemism referring to brothels or districts where brothels are concentrated. The imagery of willow-lined paths and flowering bushes evokes the pleasure quarters of old. It is used in literature to describe places associated with courtesans and entertainment.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom originates from the ci poem 'Fengliuzi' by the Song dynasty poet Lu You (陆游). In the poem, Lu You reflects on life's unpredictability, lamenting with sorrow that one might unexpectedly find oneself fallen into the pleasure quarters. The phrase 'willow paths and flower bushes' thus became a enduring literary euphemism for brothels and entertainment districts in classical Chinese writing.

💡 Example Usage

他年轻时不知上进，终日混迹于柳陌花丛之中，荒废了大好前程。

In his youth, lacking ambition, he spent his days drifting among the pleasure quarters, squandering his bright future.
