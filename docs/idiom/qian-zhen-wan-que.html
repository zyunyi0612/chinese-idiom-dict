千真万确

qiān zhēn wàn què
Literal: "thousand, true, ten thousand, certain"
Home › Clarity › 千真万确

📖 Meaning

An expression used to emphasize that something is absolutely true and beyond any doubt. It conveys complete certainty about the accuracy or authenticity of a claim or situation.

📜 Origin Story

The phrase appears in modern Chinese literature, notably cited by the writer Ying Xiuren in the work 'Jinzi Ta Yinbao Ta,' where a southern villager insists that a piece of news is absolutely true. It also appears in Mao Dun's novel 'Ziye' (Midnight), where a character vouches for the reliability of information about a military officer's concubine fleeing to the county town. The idiom compounds '千' (a thousand) and '万' (ten thousand) to signify an overwhelming degree of certainty.

💡 Example Usage

这件事千真万确，我亲眼所见。

This matter is absolutely true; I saw it with my own eyes.
