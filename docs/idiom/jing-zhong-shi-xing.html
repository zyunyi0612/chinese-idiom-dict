井中视星

jǐng zhōng shì xīng
Literal: "well, in, look at, star"
Home › Wisdom › 井中视星

📖 Meaning

Looking at stars from the bottom of a well. It is a metaphor for a narrow, limited perspective and shallow understanding. The imagery conveys how one's vantage point restricts what one can perceive of the broader world.

📜 Origin Story

The idiom originates from the pre-Qin text Shizi (《尸子》), attributed to Shi Jiao, a scholar of the Warring States period. In the chapter Guangshi (广释), it is written that by viewing stars from inside a well, one can see only a few of them—not because one's eyesight is poorer, but because the confined position limits the view. The Qing dynasty scholar Chen Li later quoted this passage in his Dongshu Dushu Ji (《东塾读书记》), using it to illustrate how circumstance, rather than ability, shapes what one can perceive.

💡 Example Usage

若只读一家之言，无异于井中视星，难以窥见学问之全貌。

If one reads only the works of a single school, it is like looking at stars from a well—hardly able to glimpse the full scope of knowledge.
