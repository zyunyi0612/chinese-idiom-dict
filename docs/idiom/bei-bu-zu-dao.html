卑不足道

bēi bù zú dào
Literal: "lowly, not, worth, to mention"
Home › Status › 卑不足道

📖 Meaning

Describes someone or something so lowly and insignificant that it is not worth mentioning. Often used in self-deprecating speech to belittle one's own status, abilities, or contributions.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom crystallized from the older form '卑卑不足道' found in late-Qing and early-Republican vernacular prose, and has no single classical anecdote attached to it. It is a compounding of 卑 (lowly, humble) with the formula 不足道 (not worth speaking of), a pattern already common in Ming–Qing literary Chinese. The phrase became widely familiar through modern literary usage, most notably in Ba Jin's 1931 novel 'Family' (《家》), where a character humbly calls himself 卑不足道. Today it serves both as modest self-reference and as a dismissive judgment of others.

💡 Example Usage

在您面前，我这点成就实在卑不足道。

Before you, my small achievements are truly not worth mentioning.
