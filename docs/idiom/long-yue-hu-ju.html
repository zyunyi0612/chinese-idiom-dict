龙跃虎踞

lóng yuè hǔ jù
Literal: "dragon, leap, tiger, crouch"
Home › Quality › 龙跃虎踞

📖 Meaning

This idiom describes something extraordinary, transcendent, and magnificent in its vigor and grandeur. It evokes the imagery of a dragon soaring upward and a tiger crouching powerfully to convey an aura of heroic majesty and imposing strength. It is often applied to calligraphy, literary style, or a person's bearing that displays exceptional power and spirit.

📜 Origin Story

The idiom draws on two of the most potent symbols in Chinese culture—the dragon (龙), representing soaring celestial power, and the tiger (虎), representing grounded terrestrial strength. The pairing of 跃 (to leap) and 踞 (to crouch or sit) creates a dynamic contrast between upward motion and firm stance, together expressing an aura that is at once unrestrained and commanding. Similar combinations of dragon and tiger imagery appear throughout classical Chinese literature, though no single definitive source is recorded for this particular formulation. It belongs to the tradition of using animal metaphors to characterize the spirit and force of artistic expression or heroic character.

💡 Example Usage

他的书法笔力遒劲，有龙跃虎踞之势。

His calligraphy is vigorous and forceful, carrying the majestic momentum of a leaping dragon and a crouching tiger.
