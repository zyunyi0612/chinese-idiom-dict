村生泊长

cūn shēng bó cháng
Literal: "village, born, lake-shore, grow"
Home › Status › 村生泊长

📖 Meaning

Refers to being born and raised in the countryside or wilderness, where '泊' denotes a lake or marsh area. It describes someone of humble rural origin, often lacking formal upbringing or education. The phrase carries a tone of condescension toward rustic, untutored backgrounds.

📜 Origin Story

The idiom originates from Act 1 of the Ming Dynasty anonymous play '孟母三移' (Mencius's Mother Moves Three Times), a dramatization of the classical story of Mencius's mother relocating three times to find a proper environment for her son's education. In the play, a character contrasts a noble descendant of a princely house with those who are 'village-born and lake-shore-raised,' lacking any proper instruction. The line underscores the Confucian theme that environment and upbringing shape a person's character and prospects.

💡 Example Usage

他虽村生泊长，却自幼勤学不辍，终成一代名儒。

Though born and raised in the countryside, he studied tirelessly from childhood and eventually became a renowned scholar of his generation.
