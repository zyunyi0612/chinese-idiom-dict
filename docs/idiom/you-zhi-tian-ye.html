有枝添叶

yǒu zhī tiān yè
Literal: "have, branch, add, leaf"
Home › Communication › 有枝添叶

📖 Meaning

This idiom describes the act of embellishing or exaggerating when recounting events or relaying someone else's words, adding details that were not originally present. It is similar to the English expression 'adding branches and leaves,' meaning to pad a story with fabricated elements. The phrase conveys a mildly critical tone toward those who distort facts through embellishment.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom draws from the natural imagery of a tree: when one already has branches, adding leaves makes the whole appear more lush and elaborate than it truly is. It uses this botanical metaphor to describe how people inflate stories by grafting on fictitious details. Unlike many classical idioms, it does not trace back to a single literary source or historical anecdote. It belongs to vernacular folk speech that crystallized into a fixed expression over time. Its logic parallels other Chinese idioms that use tree imagery to represent the growth of embellishment.

💡 Example Usage

他讲起这件事来有枝添叶，把原本简单的小事说得惊天动地。

He told the story with added embellishments, blowing a simple matter up into something earth-shattering.
