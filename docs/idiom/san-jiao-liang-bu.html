三脚两步

sān jiǎo liǎng bù
Literal: "three, feet, two, steps"
Home › Action › 三脚两步

📖 Meaning

Describes walking very quickly with light, rapid steps. It can also refer to a short distance that can be covered in just a few strides.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom originates from the Qing dynasty novel "Guānchǎng Xiànxíng Jì" (《官场现形记》, "The Bureaucrats: A Mirror of Officialdom") by Li Baojia (李宝嘉), in Chapter 10. The original passage depicts a wife hurrying into a room with quick, nimble steps, immediately followed by her cousin Zhou Daquan (周大权) bringing in the luggage, while a maid busily set about finding a comb and washing basin for her. The phrase vividly captures the brisk, hurried gait of someone moving with urgency.

💡 Example Usage

他三脚两步赶到校门口，正好赶上放学铃声。

He hurried to the school gate in quick, light steps and just caught the dismissal bell.
