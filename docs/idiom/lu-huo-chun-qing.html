炉火纯青

lú huǒ chún qīng
Literal: "furnace, fire, pure, cyan"
Home › Skill › 炉火纯青

📖 Meaning

Describes a skill, technique, or accomplishment that has been refined to a state of perfect maturity. The image comes from Daoist alchemy, where a furnace burning with a pure blue-green flame was proof that the elixir had been successfully refined. It is now used to praise attainment so consummate that it appears effortless and flawless.

📜 Origin Story

The phrase traces to the Tang dynasty physician and Daoist Sun Simiao, whose 'Four-Character Poem' describes a great furnace and blazing fire, noting that before the smoke has blackened the flame takes on no false blue-green color, a sign the firing is still incomplete. In the Daoist alchemical tradition, a pure cyan flame in the furnace signaled that the elixir was finished and the work had succeeded. The image later left the alchemy lab and became a general metaphor for any craft or accomplishment brought to its highest refinement. The Qing novelist Zeng Pu used it in 'The Flower of the Sea of Sins' to say that the craft had reached its perfect season and it was now time for each man to show his own skill.

💡 Example Usage

他的书法已到炉火纯青的地步，每一笔都从容自然。

His calligraphy has reached a state of consummate mastery, every stroke effortless and natural.
