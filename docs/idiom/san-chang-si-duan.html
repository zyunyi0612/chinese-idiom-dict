三长四短

sān cháng sì duǎn
Literal: "three long, four short"
Home › Condition › 三长四短

📖 Meaning

A euphemism for an unexpected death, disaster, or fatal accident, essentially a variant of the more common idiom 三长两短. It can also refer to gossip, slander, or unkind words spoken behind someone's back.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom is a variant of the more common expression 三长两短 (three long, two short), which is believed to originate from the traditional construction of a Chinese coffin. A coffin consists of six boards: the bottom and lid are the two 'long' pieces, while the head, foot, and two side boards make up the four 'short' pieces. The idiom substitutes 'four short' for 'two short,' subtly altering the metaphor while retaining the association with a coffin and thus with death. No specific classical text or author is credited as its source, and it appears to have emerged organically in vernacular speech.

💡 Example Usage

老人家年纪大了，万一有个三长四短，儿女们要提前做好准备。

The elderly person is getting on in years; should anything untoward happen, the children should prepare in advance.
