山止川行

shān zhǐ chuān xíng
Literal: "mountain, stop, river, flow"
Home › Determination › 山止川行

📖 Meaning

This idiom pairs two images from nature—mountains that stand immovable and rivers that flow ceaselessly onward—to describe what is both unshakeable and unstoppable. It metaphorically represents an indomitable will that cannot be destroyed and a relentless momentum that cannot be hindered. It is often used to praise firm resolve and perseverance in the face of adversity.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom does not trace to a single classical text but draws on the timeless imagery of nature central to Chinese literary tradition. The phrase contrasts two forces: mountains, which stand resolute and immovable, and rivers, which flow onward regardless of obstacles in their path. Together they convey a dual quality of unshakable resolve and unstoppable momentum. Such parallel natural metaphors are a hallmark of Chinese rhetorical style, frequently employed to extol virtue, determination, and strength of character.

💡 Example Usage

他的意志如山止川行，任何困难都无法动摇。

His willpower was like mountains standing firm and rivers flowing onward—no difficulty could shake it.
