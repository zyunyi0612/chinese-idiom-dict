慰情胜无

wèi qíng shèng wú
Literal: "comfort, feeling, surpass, nothing"
Home › Emotion › 慰情胜无

📖 Meaning

To console oneself with the thought that having something is better than having nothing at all. It serves as a phrase of self-consolation or rationalization. Often used when accepting a less-than-ideal outcome as preferable to complete emptiness or failure.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom does not have a specific classical literary origin story associated with a particular dynasty or historical figure. It functions primarily as a self-consoling expression, reflecting a philosophical resignation that partial gain or modest comfort is preferable to total loss. The phrase embodies a pragmatic acceptance of imperfect circumstances, a sentiment common in everyday Chinese discourse rather than in canonical classical texts.

💡 Example Usage

虽然奖金不多，但慰情胜无，总比两手空空强。

Though the bonus was small, it was better than nothing—at least it offered some consolation.
