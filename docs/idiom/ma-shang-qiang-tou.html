马上墙头

mǎ shàng qiáng tóu
Literal: "on horseback, on the wall"
Home › Emotion › 马上墙头

📖 Meaning

Literally 'on horseback and atop a wall,' this idiom refers to the place where young men and women meet and fall in love. It evokes the classic image of a young man riding by on horseback and a young woman leaning over a garden wall, their eyes meeting across the distance. It is used to describe romantic encounters or lovers' trysts.

📜 Origin Story

The imagery originates in Tang poetry, notably Bai Juyi's poem 'Drawing a Silver Bottle from a Well Bottom,' which contains the line 'From the wall top and horseback they gazed at each other afar; one glance, and she knew him and gave him her heart.' The phrase was later crystallized as 马上墙头 by the Northern Song poet Chao Duanli in his lyric 'Water Dragon' (水龙吟): 'On horseback, at the wall top, even if they catch a glimpse, they can hardly recognize each other.' The Qing poet Xie Xuechong also used it in his 'Niannu Jiao' lyric, showing its continued use as a byword for lovers' meetings.

💡 Example Usage

才子佳人，马上墙头，一见倾心。

The talented scholar and the lovely maiden met as if at the wall's edge, falling in love at first sight.
