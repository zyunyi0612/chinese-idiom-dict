不以为耻

bù yǐ wéi chǐ
Literal: "not, take, as, shame"
Home › Morality › 不以为耻

📖 Meaning

To not consider something shameful; to feel no sense of disgrace. It describes someone who lacks a proper sense of shame or morality, often behaving improperly without any remorse.

📜 Origin Story

The phrase originates from the philosophical text《邓析子·转辞》(Deng Xi Zi: Turning Words), attributed to Deng Xi, a thinker of the Spring and Autumn period. The original passage observes that when people subjected to punishment such as tattooing or nose-cutting no longer feel ashamed, society falls into chaos with more disorder than order. The idiom thus critiques the erosion of moral standards and the loss of a sense of shame in society.

💡 Example Usage

他抄袭别人的文章还四处炫耀，真是不以为耻。

He plagiarized someone else's article and even showed it off everywhere, truly feeling no shame about it.
