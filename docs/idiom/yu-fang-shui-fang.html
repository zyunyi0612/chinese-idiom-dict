盂方水方

yú fāng shuǐ fāng
Literal: "bowl, square, water, square"
Home › Leadership › 盂方水方

📖 Meaning

Just as water takes the shape of the bowl that holds it, people mold their conduct to the example set by those in authority. The idiom describes how a leader's words, actions, and character directly shape the prevailing morals, customs, and atmosphere of the group or nation beneath them.

📜 Origin Story

The phrase originates from the chapter 'Jun Dao' (On the Way of the Lord) in the work Xunzi, written by the Warring States Confucian philosopher Xunzi (c. 310–235 BCE). The full passage reads: 'The ruler is a basin; if the basin is round, the water is round; the ruler is a bowl; if the bowl is square, the water is square.' Xunzi used this vivid metaphor to warn rulers that their personal conduct is reflected in the character of their subjects, and that good governance must begin with the ruler's own virtue.

💡 Example Usage

正所谓盂方水方，主管以身作则，团队风气自然端正。

As the saying goes, the bowl shapes the water—when a manager leads by example, the team's culture naturally follows suit.
