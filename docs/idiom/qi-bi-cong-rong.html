弃笔从戎

qì bǐ cóng róng
Literal: "abandon, pen, join, army"
Home › Determination › 弃笔从戎

📖 Meaning

A variant of the idiom 投笔从戎, it refers to a scholar abandoning literary pursuits to join the military. The phrase describes someone giving up a civilian or scholarly life to pursue a military career, often out of patriotism or ambition.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom derives from the story of Ban Chao (班超) of the Eastern Han dynasty, who worked as a lowly scribe copying government documents. Believing that a true man should seek glory on the battlefield rather than waste his life at a desk, he threw down his brush and enlisted in the army under General Dou Gu. Ban Chao went on to achieve legendary military and diplomatic success securing the Western Regions for the Han empire. The phrase became a lasting symbol of abandoning scholarship for martial ambition. The variant 弃笔从戎 carries the same meaning as the more common 投笔从戎.

💡 Example Usage

面对国家危难，他毅然弃笔从戎，奔赴前线保家卫国。

Facing the nation's peril, he resolutely abandoned his scholarly life and joined the army, rushing to the front to defend his country.
