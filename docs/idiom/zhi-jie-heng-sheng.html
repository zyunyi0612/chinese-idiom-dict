枝节横生

zhī jié héng shēng
Literal: "branch, knot, horizontal, grow"
Home › Condition › 枝节横生

📖 Meaning

This idiom describes unexpected complications, side issues, or obstacles that suddenly and unexpectedly arise during the course of handling a matter. It conveys the frustration of a straightforward situation becoming tangled with unforeseen difficulties, much like branches and knots sprouting chaotically from a tree trunk.

📜 Origin Story

The idiom originates from a memorial to the throne written by Xue Fucheng (薛福成), a prominent Qing dynasty diplomat and essayist, titled 'A Comprehensive Plan for Establishing Consular Offices in the South Sea Islands to Protect Chinese Nationals.' In this document, Xue argued that when legal cases involving foreigners occurred, consular officials from foreign powers handled them independently, often undermining the authority of local Chinese magistrates. He pointed out that this extraterritorial interference was precisely the root cause of the numerous complications and troubles that had arisen at China's various treaty ports.

💡 Example Usage

本以为事情简单，没想到枝节横生，反而更难处理了。

I thought it would be a simple matter, but unexpected complications kept cropping up, making it even harder to handle.
