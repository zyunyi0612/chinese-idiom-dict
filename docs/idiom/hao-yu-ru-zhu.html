好语如珠

hǎo yǔ rú zhū
Literal: "good, words, like, pearls"
Home › Communication › 好语如珠

📖 Meaning

Fine phrases and brilliant remarks are as numerous as pearls strung together. It describes writing or speech filled with quotable, witty lines and memorable expressions. The idiom praises the abundance of excellent passages in a literary work.

📜 Origin Story

This idiom originates from a poem by Su Shi (苏轼), one of the greatest literary figures of the Song Dynasty. In his poem '次韵答子由' (Replying in Matching Rhyme to Ziyou), Su Shi wrote '好语如珠串一一，妄心如膜退重重,' meaning that fine words are like pearls strung one by one, while deluded thoughts retreat layer by layer like a membrane peeling away. The phrase was later adopted as a standalone idiom to describe an abundance of brilliant expressions in writing.

💡 Example Usage

这位作家笔下好语如珠，令人读来赏心悦目。

This author's writing brims with brilliant phrases, making it a delight to read.
